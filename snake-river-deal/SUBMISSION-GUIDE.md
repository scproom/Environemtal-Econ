# Week Seven submission guide

Public proposition: https://scproom.github.io/Environemtal-Econ/snake-river-deal/

Ballot: https://scproom.github.io/Environemtal-Econ/snake-river-deal/vote.html

The existing Google Form is embedded. Do not create a second poll, change the question mid-election, or submit test votes.

## Prepared materials

- index.html: seven required sections, sources and optional discounting illustration.
- vote.html: neutral explanation and live anonymous ballot.
- poster.html and public-poster.pdf: one-page Letter poster with a local QR code.
- report.html: enter real counts and comments, calculate shares, download a text report. Nothing is saved or transmitted automatically.
- submission-packet.pdf: proposition, source notes, results worksheet and reflection prompts. PDFs are also in output/pdf locally.

## Real fieldwork

1. Scan the QR with your phone and check the public page and ballot without submitting a fake vote.
2. Print the PDF at actual size, Letter. For HTML printing use no margins, background graphics on, headers/footers off.
3. Display in an acceptable public location for one week. Record actual dates and photograph it hanging there.
4. Recruit at least 12 different people. Ask them to vote once without steering the answer. Anonymous submissions alone cannot verify distinct participants.
5. Keep the poll open during display. Use the form owner's Responses tab for actual counts and comments; the public site cannot read private responses.
6. Enter counts and anonymized verbatim comments in report.html. Keep YES, IF separate from YES. Download before closing the page.
7. Submit to Blackboard: actual poster photo, public proposition/ballot link, counts and shares, interesting comments, and short reflection.

## X-style reflection scaffold — not observed results

1/ I asked [N] people whether to breach four Snake River dams with up to $20B for replacements. The bargain puts salmon recovery against clean power, shipping and public spending.

2/ [Y]% said YES, [N]% NO and [C]% YES, IF. A revealing condition was: “[short actual quote]”.

3/ That response suggests [evidence-supported lesson about preferences]. I would revise [specific term] because [reason grounded in comments].

4/ This small convenience sample cannot speak for the public. What it changed in my thinking was [your actual reflection].

Replace every bracket after fieldwork; keep each finished post within 280 characters. Do not infer motives solely from totals.
