## The idea in brief

- **The problem:** tourism produces about 8% of global greenhouse gas emissions (4.5 billion tonnes CO₂e), and current policy leaves much of it uncovered.
- **Why taxes fall short:** travel demand is inelastic; a steep frequent-flyer tax would cut flying only about 7%.
- **The substitute:** a brain simulation records a real person's experience and simulates it for you, first-person, at roughly 0.25 kg CO₂ per session versus about 1 tonne for a transatlantic round trip. Sessions could be shared, like a multiplayer game.
- **Can we build it?** Today's VR covers sight and sound; brain implants are early. Cochlear implants show the brain can use crude signals.
- **Payoff and price:** replacing 5–20% of leisure trips would avoid 225–900 million tonnes of CO₂e a year, worth $43–171B in avoided climate damage, while a 10% shift puts about 37 million tourism jobs at risk.
- **Public reaction:** 78% of Americans say they would not want a brain chip, so the path starts with headsets and medical implants and stays reversible.
